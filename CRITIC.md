## Bottom line (for the trader)
The desk is flat because of its rules, not because the math says the trade is bad. The math says something weaker: it cannot tell. Every EV on Oct 8 was within one standard error of zero, the market priced the 1DTE spreads near fair, and the 29 historical analogs are a coin flip. "No measurable edge" is a fair reading of that, and the EV layer is not rigged against you. The verdict layer is: TAKE needs EV > 0 at p_t − 10pp while p_t is capped at p_mkt + 10pp, so TAKE needs the market to be mispriced; the grade gate scores missing data as zero; EV_B and the cap act one way only. Those rules turn "can't tell" into "no" by construction. That is a defensible policy for a small account. It is not a calculation, and it should be labelled as policy.

Two things in the report cut the other way from how it reads them. First, "SKIP was right 72%" is empty: 72% is the loss rate of OTM debit structures in the sample, and SKIP rows won at the same rate as every other row (28.0% vs 28.2%). On the 1DTE rows, always-take was +1% to +14% of debit (noise, but positive) and WATCH rows were +18% of debit; the negative dollar means come from the Oct 12/16 rows, one of which is a straw man. Second, Oct 8 proves nothing either way: it was a ~1.25-ATR, 1.6–1.8-sd tail day, and under the symmetric 5m-close stop the brief's own trigger implies, the +480% trade is a −17% stop-out at 13:45. The only thing that will answer the trader's question is 30–60 forward ledger rows with a decision rule fixed today.

## 1. Does the calibration support "flat was right"? No.
- **Hit rate is base rate.** "SKIP right" means realized P&L ≤ 0. 72% of all 142 rows lost; SKIP was "right" 72%. SKIP win rate 28.0%, all rows 28.2%: zero discrimination. LOO is the same (SKIP 30% vs 27% overall). A rule that said SKIP on every row would score 72%.
- **Dollars vs percent.** The report says WATCH "lost on average (−$18)". The same JSON has WATCH at **+18% of debit** (the rows are cheap Oct9 375C). The daily-breakout section switches to % of debit when that reads better. Fix one metric before looking; for convex structures it should be summed counterfactual P&L in % of debit, not hit rate.
- **Horizon mix.** Always-taken, per structure: Oct9 375C +13%, 370/375C +1%, 370/380C +11%, 375/385C +14%; Oct12 370/380C −5%; Oct16 375/385C −44%. The 1DTE setup, the thing under test, was flat-to-positive. Oct16 375/385C (a 6-session spread with a 0.23-ATR daily-close stop, stopped 23/28 times) is not a structure anyone trades; it supplies 21 of the 85 "SKIP right" rows. Without it, 64/96 = 67%, still base rate.
- **n.** 24 events, not 142 decisions: the six structures per event share one path. The "6 TAKEs that all lost" are 4 days (4× Oct9 375C, 2× 375/385C); P(4 losses | p_win = 1/3) ≈ 0.20. Their mean model EV was +$105 on $127–172 debits: that is the expanding-window prior at n = 5–10 being overconfident, not evidence about the gates. With intraday stops the all-rows mean improves from −$29 to −$10 while the win rate halves, so even the sign of "flat was right" depends on the stop rule.
- **Look-ahead in the design.** The event detector (daily pivots ±0.3%, depth ≤ 0.4 ATR, reclaim ≤ 2 bars, entry 09:45–15:30), the 13-structure grid 370–390 bracketing a path that ended at 388.55, the 375/385C bolded in RA's file at R:R 6.45, and the 15m sample as primary were all chosen by people who had seen the close (brief, method files, daily-bar print, RA's web search). The SHA proves the file did not change after 13:17:55 Oct 9, 21 hours after the outcome; it does not make the design blind. RA's invalidation ("5m close under 369") sits 0.88 below the only post-cutoff 5m close (369.88) that a symmetric 370.25 rule stops on; whether or not it was chosen that way, parameters like this must be fixed before the event. Treat phase 1 as a reproducible reconstruction, and treat the Feb–Oct 2026 15m sample as burned for validation.

Netting: the toward-flat rules cost this row $0 of EV and one verdict notch; the toward-trade modelling added about $45–50 of EV and $50 of EV_B. Honest EV is about −$10 without convexity and about +$40 with it, standard error $40–60 either way. The report's "they roughly cancel" is right in dollars and wrong in kind: the toward-trade items are measurement errors in EV, the toward-flat items are gates that never touch EV. More important, **the claim that the grade gate was the binding rule holds only in the flat-vol specification the report itself calls an artifact.** Under the report's own calibrated market measure the band fails (EV at −10pp = −$12); under the trader's stop the EV_B gate fails (−$15). Three gates block the same row under three honest specifications; it is a TAKE only when two artifacts (band floored at p_t = 0, smile-cheapened market EV) line up with grade B. RA's mechanism 1 is correct algebra: the rules are flat-biased by construction, the EV is roughly unbiased, and the right description is "no edge found, and the rules cannot say TAKE without one".

## 3. The fixes
- **Fix 1, renormalise the grade over scored factors: right in principle, dangerous as written.** 0.55 / 0.90 = 0.61 ≥ 0.60 → B → TAKE on Oct 8. A change whose first effect is to flip the one case with a known outcome is post-hoc by definition. Also 0.20 of the 0.55 is liquidity +2, a cost term, not an edge signal, and renormalising over fewer factors inflates the grade's variance. v2: move liquidity to the cost model; renormalise; a grade with < 70% of weight scored is "insufficient" and cannot gate; the grade stops gating TAKE and becomes a size multiplier (0.5× / 1×) until the ledger shows it predicts outcomes.
- **Fix 2, model the stop actually traded: correct, and the only fix that removes degrees of freedom.** One stop rule per setup class, declared before the event, used identically in the event study, the market MC, realized P&L and the ledger. Note its effect here: the brief's symmetric 5m rule turns the +480% into −17%; RA's 15m / 369 rule keeps it. The stop choice, not the gates, decided whether Oct 8 was a win.
- **Fix 3, make EV_B symmetric: half right.** Promotion adds a second noisy estimate that can mint TAKEs; the no-cap/min/demotion variant is what produced the six losers. The +17%-of-debit demoted rows are bull-sample drift, which the report concedes two lines later. Make EV_B a reported diagnostic, not a gate, in either direction. Fewer gates, not mirrored gates.
- Fixes 4–7: the n-scaled band and k = 30 blend are fine if k is fixed now and never tuned. Fix 5 (full payoff, smile-consistent market) is a consistency fix; it works against the report's own headline. Fix 6 (intraday module): 2 of ~20 in-sample tests cleared a 95% CI, about the chance count, and 4 of the 6 "signals" favour Oct 8; keep it out of any probability until validated forward. Fix 7, the ledger, is the one that matters.

**Minimal v2, pre-registerable today.** Keep: prior → shrink → market MC → EV; exit costs; settle-based probabilities; the correlation bucket; the "history ≈ drift" line. Change: (a) one stop rule per setup class; (b) market MC vol calibrated to the quoted debit, with a unit test that market EV = −costs on every row; (c) full payoff valuation for ≤ 2 DTE; (d) one decision statistic, EV_blend at p = (n·p_hist + k·p_mkt)/(n + k), k = 30, replacing cap, band, EV_B gate and grade gate: TAKE if EV_blend > max(3% of debit, 1 s.e.), WATCH if > 0, else SKIP; (e) until the ledger validates, every TAKE is a PAPER-TAKE at fixed size. Ledger: one row per event per setup class (headline structure chosen by a fixed rule, not six correlated rows) with time, structure, full debit, stop-rule id, p_hist, p_mkt, EV_blend, verdict, reason code, and counterfactual P&L at settle/stop, +1 session, and 50%-of-max TP. Decision rule at N = 30 events per class: bootstrap the mean counterfactual P&L (% of debit) of flat calls; CI above 0 → loosen one pre-specified notch (k 30 → 15); CI straddling 0 → flat stands; CI below 0 → flat was right. Also compare Brier(p_hist) with Brier(p_mkt), and drop the history prior if the market wins. Power: with sd ≈ 150% of debit on 1DTE spreads, N = 30 detects only an edge ≳ 50% of debit and N = 60 ≳ 35–40%; write that into the pre-registration. The Feb–Oct 2026 sample is burned, the 2024–26 60m sample (n = 20) is partly burned by phase 3b, and only forward rows are clean.

## 4. Plain words
The desk is not lying about the math: on this setup it has no edge it can show, so "don't know" is the honest estimate. The desk is flat because it has wired "don't know" to mean "no", four separate ways, and because its one historical check could not have said otherwise. Oct 8 does not vindicate you (one tail day, and a win only under the looser stop), and the six paper losers do not vindicate the desk (four days). If you want the answer, run the ledger with the rule above and read it at 30 and 60 events. Until then, any TAKE from this desk would rest on a specification choice, not on evidence.
