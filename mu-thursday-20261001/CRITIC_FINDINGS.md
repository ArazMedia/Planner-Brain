## Why (a) fails: the drift is real but small, and two legs × two fills cost more than it pays
- **Direction (short R0 critique — verdict on the drift: WEAKENED, real but small; the stock short itself is out of scope, no margin).** MU's day-1 open→close drift is −0.99% (t = −2.31, 63% of 98 prints negative, median −1.1%). But it is weaker than the headline: the 2001–15 half has t = −1.1, and the build script dropped the 2002-06-25 print on a 07:00 timestamp although the next session (−13% gap, 1.6× volume) shows an after-close print — a −1.07R stop-out, so R0 is +0.32R on n = 99. Across 19 peers (1,398 SEC-verified after-close prints; semis plus AAPL/MSFT/NFLX/TSLA/META/AMZN) the pooled day-1 drift is −0.13% (t = −1.2); 14 of 19 names are negative, none significantly, and MU is the most negative of the 20. That matches the published overnight-up/intraday-reversal effect in high-attention stocks (Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang, *JFQA* 47(4), 2012, DOI 10.1017/S0022109012000270: −0.07% to −0.7%/day by subsample; not earnings-specific). **Working assumption for Thursday: bearish intraday lean, best-guess magnitude −0.4 to −0.7% after shrinkage toward the panel, i.e. $4–8 on a $1,065 stock, concentrated in the first hour (n = 11 hourly prints: −2.0% by 10:30, +0.3% after).**
- **The option layer.** Realized vol of the horizons being bet on: day-1 open→close 67% annualized, open→Fri close 63%, Thu→Mon close 47%; forward vol implied by the 9/29 chain for Oct 2→Oct 9 is 42%. So post-crush IV ≈ 65% (Oct 2) / 45–55% (Oct 9) is fair, and a vertical's EV barely moves with IV anyway (see table). What kills it is cost: a $10-wide ATM Oct 2 vertical is worth ~$4.77 at mid (half its width) with an entry delta of only 0.06, so a $6 favourable move is worth ~$0.40 against $1.00–2.00 of half-spreads (Oct 2 ATM quoted $0.73–1.00 on 9/29, so $0.50/leg/side is the right base); held to expiry it needs MU to finish > 1% below the open just to double, and the −1% mean drift comes with a 4.2% standard deviation.
- **Budget.** At $1,065, a $10-wide near-ATM vertical costs $450–600 (fits); $20-wide costs $900–1,050 (does not fit); an ATM Oct 2 put is ~$2,400; $650 buys only a ~5% OTM Oct 2 put.

## Multiple-testing haircut
1,516 structures priced here; the desk had ~1,830 (RA) + 168 (Planner) + 6 rules before that. Bonferroni at M = 1,516 needs one-sided p < 3.3e-5 (t ≈ 4.0); at M ≈ 3,500, t ≈ 4.2; expected max |t| of that many null tests ≈ 3.8–4.0. Observed best tradeable t = 1.47 (adjusted p = 1.0). White (2000) reality check over the 326 tradeable structures, bootstrapping the 98 prints: p = 0.42 (open-entry family 0.44; fallback family 0.99). The best-looking cells (+0.25R with-gap ≥ 7%, +0.11R lottery put) are pre-2016-only and flip sign in the last 12 prints — the signature of noise.
